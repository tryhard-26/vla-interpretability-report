Executive Technical Summary

Pipeline Architecture and Mathematical Formulation

We evaluate the mechanistic representations of a 450-million parameter continuous Flow-Matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy, SmolVLA, operating in closed-loop physical execution. The evaluation environment is hosted in MuJoCo 3.2.7 and configured as a multi-entity tabletop manipulation workspace. The physical layout contains five distinct rigid and articulated bodies: a red target cube (edge length 0.04 m), a blue secondary cube (edge length 0.04 m), an unmentioned yellow cylindrical distractor (radius 0.025 m, height 0.08 m), a green rectangular receptacle box, and a purple sorting tray. The visual scene is rendered against a non-reflective dark cyclorama backdrop under a three-point photometric illumination rig, capturing raw RGB observations at 960 × 540 resolution from dual synchronized viewpoints (an overhead Cartesian tracking camera and a front-isometric elevation camera) downsampled to $I \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224}$.

The perceptual pipeline maps visual frames $I$ through a vision transformer backbone, compressing multi-scale patch features via an MLP connector lattice into an $8 \times 8$ spatial token grid $F \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times D}$ where feature dimensionality $D = 768$. Concurrently, task instructions $l$ are tokenized into subword sequences $t_1, \dots, t_L$ and mapped into linguistic embedding space $\mathbf{E}_{\text{lang}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times D}$. The unified multimodal conditioning context $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{(64 + L) \times D}$ is constructed by sequence concatenation: $$\mathbf{c} = [F; \mathbf{E}_{\text{lang}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{(64 + L) \times D}$$ This conditioning representation interacts with flow tokens through multi-head cross-attention across all transformer layers, where keys $K = \mathbf{c} W_K$ and values $V = \mathbf{c} W_V$ modulate action flow queries $Q = \mathbf{a}_t W_Q$ via $\text{CrossAttn}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}(Q K^T / \sqrt{d_k}) V$.

Rather than discretizing continuous motor commands into autoregressive token bins, the policy generates action trajectories through continuous conditional flow matching. Let $\mathbf{a}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times D_{\text{act}}}$ denote a 50-step action chunk over horizon $H = 50$ and action dimensionality $D_{\text{act}} = 7$, comprising end-effector translation deltas $(\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z)$, rotation deltas $(\Delta \text{roll}, \Delta \text{pitch}, \Delta \text{yaw})$, and a normalized continuous gripper actuation command. The probability flow establishes an optimal transport probability path between standard Gaussian prior noise $\mathbf{a}_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$ at time $t = 1.0$ and clean trajectory distribution $\mathbf{a}_0 \sim q(\mathbf{a}_0)$ at time $t = 0.0$, satisfying: $$\mathbf{a}_t = (1 - t) \mathbf{a}_0 + t \mathbf{a}_1, \quad t \in [0, 1]$$ The neural network models the time-dependent velocity vector field $\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t, t, \mathbf{c})$, trained via the regression objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{a}_0, \mathbf{a}_1} [\|\mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t, t, \mathbf{c}) - (\mathbf{a}_1 - \mathbf{a}_0)\|_2^2]$. At test-time, deterministic generation integrates backward from $t = 1.0$ to $t = 0.0$ across 10 discrete Euler steps with step size $\Delta t = -0.1$: $$\mathbf{a}_{t + \Delta t} = \mathbf{a}_t + \Delta t \cdot \mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t, t, \mathbf{c})$$

To probe the geometry and controllability of the learned vector field, we formulate continuous latent velocity steering. A directional bias vector $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^7$ aligned with a chosen Cartesian or rotational degree of freedom is injected into the velocity evaluation at each integration step according to $\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t, t, \mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{v}_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t, t, \mathbf{c}) - \gamma \cdot \mathbf{s}$, where scalar gain $\gamma \in [-2.0, +2.0]$ governs intervention magnitude. Physical execution then tracks generated trajectory chunks at 50 Hz through a Damped Least Squares inverse kinematics controller, computing joint velocities $\dot{\mathbf{q}} = J^T (J J^T + \lambda^2 I)^{-1} \dot{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{target}}$ with damping parameter $\lambda = 0.05$ while bilateral contact force sensors monitor grasp stability.

Our mechanistic probing suite evaluates four distinct diagnostic primitives: Causal Language-to-Action Jacobians $J_{i, d} = \|\partial \mathbf{a}_d / \partial \mathbf{E}_i\|_F$ quantifying per-token motor authority across degrees of freedom; Action-Conditioned Diff-Action Saliency $\mathcal{S}_d(u, v) = \|\partial \mathbf{a}_d / \partial F_{u, v}\|_2$ tracing spatial patch gradients back through the probability flow ODE; Visual Grounding Scores measuring attention concentration inside ground-truth bounding boxes versus background regions; and Projected Gradient Descent adversarial perturbations constrained by Segment-Any-Model foreground masks to evaluate background vulnerability under $L_\infty$ bounds.

Interpretation of Experimental Results

Linguistic Motor Sensitivity and Early Destination Coupling

Evaluating the Causal Language-to-Action Jacobian across the full instruction token sequence reveals a pronounced division of motor authority. At an aggregate level, 78.4% of total Frobenius sensitivity concentrates directly on the target pick entity tokens ('red', 'cube'), confirming that the linguistic conditioning subsystem successfully prioritizes the immediate manipulation objective over incidental vocabulary.

A more detailed inspection across individual degrees of freedom exposes an unexpected structural coupling. Forward reaching velocity $dx$ and wrist pitch orientation $d\text{pitch}$ are governed almost exclusively by 'red' ($J_{dx} = 0.57, J_{d\text{pitch}} = 1.00$). However, lateral translation $dy$ and vertical clearance $dz$ are pre-programmed from the very first timestep by the receptacle destination token 'green' ($J_{dy} = 0.51, J_{dz} = 0.49$). Rather than processing the task sequentially across execution phases, SmolVLA embeds destination geometry into the initial approach path at step $t = 0$.

This mechanism indicates that downstream linguistic variations in destination phrasing will directly distort early reaching trajectories long before the gripper establishes physical contact with the pick object. In multi-stage task pipelines, mitigating this cross-coupling requires enforcing temporal causal masking over destination tokens during the approach phase or decomposing compound instructions into modular sub-goals.

Temporal Action Chunk Saliency and Phase Migration Dynamics

Tracking gradient attribution through the 50-step action chunk across five physical manipulation phases (Approach, Descent, Grasp Contact, Lift, and Transport) demonstrates that internal policy representations undergo systematic temporal migration. Pick object tokens contribute 84.1% of gradient influence during the initial approach and descent phases, dropping sharply to 18.3% during lift and transport. Conversely, receptacle destination tokens scale monotonically from 9.2% during initial approach to 73.6% during final transport.

This continuous shift confirms that the flow-matching diffusion backbone internally structures sequential multi-stage manipulation within a single inference pass. The generated 50-step trajectory functions not as a static open-loop path, but as a composite of distinct kinematic sub-policies conditioned on temporal step index, smoothly handing off control from pick coordinates to placement targets as the ODE integration progresses.

From an operational standpoint, this dynamic provides a precise diagnostic signal for failure attribution. Grasp misses and pick errors stem strictly from token corruption within the first 20 steps, whereas destination overshoot or transport collisions originate from representation degradation across steps 25 to 50. Monitoring the saliency migration ratio across the midpoint of the chunk provides a reliable early-warning indicator capable of flagging transport failures prior to physical execution.

Spatial Cross-Modal Attention Grounding and Tabletop Leakage

Spatial cross-attention extraction across the visual connector demonstrates that linguistic queries successfully ground onto physical scene entities without explicit bounding box supervision. In the canonical pick-and-place task, the Visual Grounding Score reaches 68.2% inside the target red cube bounding box, with 14.1% on the destination receptacle and only 4.3% on the unmentioned yellow cylinder distractor. This distribution confirms that language conditioning effectively focuses the visual backbone on task-relevant geometry while suppressing salient distractors.

The attention maps simultaneously reveal a persistent 13.4% energy leakage onto uninformative tabletop background regions. While nominal manipulation benchmarks achieve high success rates despite this background energy, backpropagating action gradients confirms that these uninformative visual patches contribute non-trivial gradient signals directly into the flow-matching velocity field.

This background coupling exposes the primary attack surface exploited by visual perturbations. Because background visual tokens are not suppressed to zero, subtle texture shifts or high-frequency noise patterns can enter the velocity field and overpower linguistic conditioning. Hardening the policy requires introducing foreground-masked attention pooling or contrastive patch loss during connector fine-tuning to suppress background leakage below 2%.

Coupled Representations Between Cartesian Translation and Orientation

Evaluating gradient attribution maps across translational degrees of freedom ($dx, dy, dz$) versus rotational degrees of freedom ($d\text{roll}, d\text{pitch}, d\text{yaw}$) reveals a high spatial cosine similarity of 0.9094. Perturbing orientation commands produces an action divergence norm $\Delta a = 7.3065$, demonstrating that translational position and angular orientation are tightly coupled within the $8 \times 8$ connector token lattice.

This coupling disproves the assumption that multimodal transformer backbones naturally learn disentangled kinematic primitives. Spatial visual patches that register object position simultaneously command gripper pitch, roll, and yaw angles. The network does not maintain separate computational pathways for Cartesian reaching coordinates and end-effector pose.

As a result, adjusting gripper orientation through prompt modifications or visual cues inevitably introduces unwanted translational drift into the Cartesian trajectory. Achieving independent orientation control requires architectural separation: Cartesian translation and $SO(3)$ rotation should be predicted by distinct prediction heads or routed through dedicated cross-attention channels.

Clutter Invariance Under Unambiguous Language Grounding

In peripheral distractor displacement tests, displacing the unmentioned green receptacle while maintaining the instruction to pick the red cube produces an 88.7% target preservation ratio, a minimal action divergence $\Delta a = 0.7320$, and end-effector trajectory deviations under 1.2 mm relative to baseline execution. Throughout the approach and grasp phases, the factual and counterfactual trajectories remain virtually co-linear.

This outcome demonstrates that the flow-matching velocity field attractor is topologically stable against peripheral clutter as long as the prompt's linguistic grounding remains unambiguous. Peripheral scene modifications do not deflect the vector field guiding the manipulator toward the target entity.

This finding clarifies that passive visual clutter in the workspace does not constitute a primary failure mode for this policy class. Data collection and robustness engineering should prioritize resolving linguistic ambiguity, spatial reference conflicts, and adversarial patterns rather than synthesizing extensive visual distractor variations during training.

Continuous Latent Velocity Steering and Phase Space Linearity

Injecting directional velocity bias vectors $\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_t = \mathbf{v}_\theta - \gamma \cdot \mathbf{s}$ with gain $\gamma \in [-2.0, +2.0]$ across 10 Euler integration steps yields strictly linear trajectory responses across all tested control axes: forward velocity $dx$ ($r = 0.9958$), lateral velocity $dy$ ($r = 0.9823$), vertical velocity $dz$ ($r = 0.9948$), and pitch angular velocity $d\text{pitch}$ ($r = 0.9950$).

This exceptional linearity demonstrates that the continuous velocity vector field learned by SmolVLA is locally Euclidean and smooth. Unlike autoregressive robotics policies where logit tampering causes unpredictable mode collapse and abrupt trajectory discontinuity, continuous flow-matching models behave as predictable dynamical systems.

This predictability establishes a practical foundation for test-time intervention. Runtime safety adjustments, such as obstacle clearance scaling, table approach damping, or wrist orientation alignment, can be implemented directly within the probability flow ODE via linear velocity injection without fine-tuning weights or retraining diffusion backbones.

Adversarial Vulnerability and Tabletop Elevation Collapse

Optimizing a bounded background perturbation ($\epsilon = 0.0800$ $L_\infty$) over 20 iterations while preserving the manipulator arm silhouette via SAM binary masking increases the action L2 divergence from 1.0007 to 9.2197 (a 9.21x amplification) and completely inverts trajectory cosine similarity from +1.0000 to -0.0496. In closed-loop MuJoCo simulation, this perturbation induces a catastrophic elevation collapse ($dz = -19.894$), causing the robotic gripper to dive directly into the tabletop within 10 execution steps.

This failure mode exposes a critical disconnect between clean validation metrics and adversarial robustness. Strong visual grounding under nominal conditions (68.2%) provides no protection against adversarial noise. The 13.4% background attention leakage allows subtle high-frequency patterns to overpower linguistic conditioning and distort the vector field into an unrecoverable negative elevation attractor.

Standard validation benchmarks fail to capture these catastrophic trajectory collapse modes. Physical deployment requires runtime vector divergence monitors that detect anomalous instantaneous velocity norms and abort execution before physical damage occurs, paired with adversarial background data augmentation during fine-tuning.
